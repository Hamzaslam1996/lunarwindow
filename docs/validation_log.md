## 2026-09-26 — Fincannon (2007): worst-case lunar-day illumination, Shackleton rim

**Status: partially reproduced.** Matched to −0.015 only at the best LOLA crest point within 3 km
of the paper's rounded coordinates *and* with a 10 m observer. At the paper's coordinates as
printed, our value is 0.23 (residual ≈ −0.48) at any height. Reasons below; full text of the paper
still to be checked for the observer height and year it used.

### Reading of the gap, largest effect first

1. **Where the site is (dominant).** The printed A1 lands 350 m below the local LOLA crest (1546 m
   vs 1736 m within 1.6 km); the paper says 1.9 km. The printed B lands in a saddle at 1214 m,
   while the LOLA connecting-ridge crest (1959 m at 89.44°S 222.9°E, matching the paper's
   1.9 km) is ~3 km away. Both are consistent with the paper's 0.1° rounding (±1.5 km in latitude
   at 89.8°S) plus the positional offset of the 2006 radar DEM relative to LOLA control. Moving
   from the printed point to the crest changes the worst-day mean from 0.23 to 0.51–0.70. The
   reference is therefore best read as "the crest of the Shackleton rim near 204–213°E", not the
   printed coordinates.
2. **Observer height.** Not stated. At the A1 crest, 2 m → 10 m moves the worst-day mean from
   0.51 to 0.695. A power-systems paper that discusses towers plausibly evaluated arrays some
   metres above the surface; if the baseline was ~10 m our number matches to 0.015. If it was
   ~2 m, a −0.20 residual remains and points at the DEM (item 3).
3. **DEM.** GSSR 2006 (40 m/px, radar, smoothed) vs LOLA GDR (20 m/px, altimetry). A smoother
   skyline drops small near-field obstructions and raises illumination; crest heights also differ
   (LOLA's highest point within 5 km of A1 is 1738 m, not 1.9 km). Expected to explain a good
   part of any residual that height does not.
4. **Disc model.** We integrate the solar disc (0.53°) against a straight skyline. A model that
   counts the Sun as "up" when its centre (or any part) clears the horizon gives higher averages
   for a grazing Sun; the difference can reach ~0.05–0.1 in the worst month. Unknown which the
   paper used.
5. **Period / anchoring.** Anchored vs sliding worst windows differ by ≤ 0.004, so anchoring is
   not a factor. The worst window falls in May–June (southern winter) every year; the 18.6-year
   nodal cycle changes the depth of that minimum by a few hundredths between years. Our 2024–2026
   window is arbitrary relative to the paper's.
6. **Ray sampling at 0 m.** At the A1 best point the coarse search (180 azimuths, 20 m step)
   gave 0.494 while the full run gave 0.339 for a 0 m observer; at 2 m and 10 m the two agree
   to < 0.005. A surface-level observer on a crest is on the knife edge of the skyline and its
   numbers are not robust to sampling. Do not quote 0 m results without this caveat; the product
   default of 2 m is stable.
